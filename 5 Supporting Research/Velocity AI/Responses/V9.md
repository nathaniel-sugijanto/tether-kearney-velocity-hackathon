I'd compare the eight products against the same BOM-to-spend workflow, rather than treat every procurement or product-costing platform as a direct substitute.

| Criterion | What I'll check |
|---|---|
| BOM-to-spend linkage | Can it connect BOM parts to PO and invoice lines across ERPs? What matching and data preparation are required? |
| Savings sizing | Does it quantify opportunities from linked spend, or primarily support adjacent work such as should-costing or sourcing? |

I'll separate documented capability from my assessment of fit. For every numerical finding, I'll give the source name, URL, date, and one of your four evidence labels. If a requested figure is unavailable, I'll say "not published" without estimating it.

Does that comparison frame work? In particular, should "savings" mean addressable purchased-part price variance from matched BOM/PO/invoice data, or should I also assess design-to-cost and supplier-negotiation opportunities?

### Our reply (drafted in the data gathering thread; Nathaniel to confirm he sent it as written)

Yes, the frame works, with two additions. Please add (1) time from signed contract to first usable output, and (2) where client data is hosted and whether it has to leave the client's systems. Those two, plus cost, are the comparisons we care about most.

On savings: use the narrow meaning as the like-for-like comparison. That is addressable purchased-part savings from matched BOM, PO and invoice data: price variance for the same part across suppliers and plants, duplicate and near-identical parts that could be consolidated, and prices paid above the awarded quote. Our tool does only that.

Treat design-to-cost, should-costing and benchmark-driven supplier negotiation as adjacent capabilities. Say which vendors offer them and where that makes them stronger than us, but do not size them. I will send five prompts, one at a time.
