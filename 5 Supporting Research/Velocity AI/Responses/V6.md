# V6 (T3-3): Shared parts and the true cost of one BOM

Asked: 2026-11-13 ~11:29 PM CT

## Prompt (sent by Nathaniel as worded in the thread)
When several BOMs share the same parts, how do practitioners allocate spend to each BOM and calculate the true cost of one product? Explain the calculation (quantity per unit, build volumes, price variance across suppliers and plants), how spare and warranty consumption is separated from production, and how to report spend that cannot be linked. Give formulas where they exist and label them as public or your own.

## Velocity response (verbatim, links as pasted)
Received: 2026-11-13 11:30 PM CT

The key distinction is purchased spend versus product cost. If three machines share a bearing, the supplier invoice belongs to the bearing—not automatically to any one machine. Practitioners first establish how many bearings each machine actually consumed, then value that consumption. A BOM-based allocation is a useful fallback when production-order consumption is unavailable, but it is not the same as actual cost.

Source labels: Public means a cited system/vendor description or formula. My calculation/inference means the analytical method I recommend for this two-plant product-family case; it is not a published Kearney PERLab formula.

### 1. Calculate demand for each shared part
Use the effective, plant-specific production BOM, exploded to purchased items. Do not count both a bought assembly and its children. Convert pack, length or weight units to a common part-specific unit before multiplying.

* My calculation — required production quantity:
`RequiredQty(part, plant, period) = Σ over products [BOMQty(part per product, plant, revision) × completed build volume(product, plant, period)]`
Adjust for configured options, approved substitutes and documented expected scrap where applicable.
* My calculation — a product's share of a shared part:
`ProductionShare(product, part, plant) = (BOMQty × completed builds) / Σ(BOMQty × completed builds)`
This is a proxy for allocating attributable production consumption when actual issues cannot be traced to orders—not a rule for dividing every PO or invoice.

For example, if product A uses two bearings and 100 are built, while product B uses one and 100 are built at the same plant, their expected requirements are 200 and 100 bearings. A defensible BOM-based proxy assigns two-thirds of that plant's production-attributable bearing consumption to A and one-third to B. It does not say that two-thirds of every bearing purchased that period was used in A: some purchases may remain in inventory or be used for service. Public product-sourcing documentation describes BOM cost rollups, and SAP production reporting distinguishes target from actual component consumption. [SAP product sourcing](https://help.sap.com/doc/d0a6d5f5cdd2447fa5af2700ce35e5a4/2608/en-US/ProductSourcing.pdf) · [SAP target/actual material consumption](https://help.sap.com/doc/saphelp_nw74/7.4.16/en-US/3e/c98853630b3d58e10000000a174cb4/content.htm?no_cache=true)

### 2. Prefer actual issues for actual product material cost
Best available basis: link inventory issues/backflushes to production orders or product cost collectors, then to the completed machines. This captures actual substitutions and usage above or below the BOM. Use the ERP's applicable inventory valuation method for the issue cost; do not assume that this month's invoice price is the cost of material issued from stock this month. SAP's production analysis, for example, reports actual material quantities and costs at order or finished-product level. [SAP production analysis](https://help.sap.com/doc/saphelp_nw74/7.4.16/en-US/3e/c98853630b3d58e10000000a174cb4/content.htm?no_cache=true)

* My calculation — actual direct-material cost per completed unit:
`Σ(production-order material-issue quantities × their booked issue values per unit, net of returns) / completed good units`
Apply this to a consistent set of orders and period; address work in process and scrap separately rather than silently dividing their costs among good units.

If order-level issues are missing: use the BOM quantities and build volumes above to estimate each product's share within each plant and period, apply a documented plant-period material price, and label the result modeled material cost, not actual product cost. Reconcile the modeled total with recorded production consumption and show the gap.

### 3. Keep plant and supplier price differences visible
The same approved part can have different prices by supplier, plant, date, currency, freight terms or purchasing unit. Normalize units and currency first; then report both the plant-specific cost and its supplier mix. Do not apply Plant 1's cheapest quote to Plant 2's actual builds unless presenting an explicit sourcing scenario.

* My calculation — observed purchase price for comparison:
`WeightedPurchasePrice(part, plant, period) = Σ(net eligible purchase-line value) / Σ(purchased quantity in common units)`
Show supplier-level prices and quantities alongside it. This is a purchase-price measure, not necessarily the inventory issue cost.
* My calculation — BOM-based modeled unit material cost:
`ModeledMaterialCost(product, plant) = Σ over purchased BOM parts [effective quantity per unit × chosen plant-specific unit price]`
* My calculation — family average, if one number is required:
`Σ(plant-specific product unit cost × completed product units at that plant) / Σ(completed product units across plants)`

State whether the "chosen price" is standard cost, actual issue valuation, historical weighted purchase price or a future supplier quote. Those answer different questions. For purchase price variance under Oracle's cited standard-cost method, the public formula is `PPV = (PO unit price − standard unit cost) × quantity received`; it is calculated at receipt and should not simply be pasted onto products that happened to be built in the same month. [Oracle standard-cost PPV](https://docs.oracle.com/cd/E26401_01/doc.122/e48829/T372621T373688.htm) Where the ERP already attributes variances to orders or inventory, use its postings and finance-approved treatment rather than inventing a second allocation.

### 4. Ring-fence spares and warranty before allocating production
Tag material movements by destination and purpose, not merely by part number:

* Production: issues to machine production orders.
* Aftermarket spare: issues or purchases for service-parts stock and customer spare orders.
* Warranty: parts consumed against warranty service orders/claims.
* Still in inventory: receipts not yet consumed, including spare stock.

A bearing used in a warranty repair may be physically identical to one in a new machine, but its consumption is not another new-machine BOM unit. Link service SKUs and supersessions to the underlying part for combined sourcing analysis, while preserving separate production, paid-service and warranty cost lines. Public SAP documentation describes service orders as recording material use and associated costs; production reports separately track production-order consumption. [SAP service orders](https://help.sap.com/doc/b183ce53118d4308e10000000a174cb4/700_SFIN3E%20006/en-US/88c7b65334e6b54ce10000000a174cb4.html) · [SAP production analysis](https://help.sap.com/doc/saphelp_nw74/7.4.16/en-US/3e/c98853630b3d58e10000000a174cb4/content.htm?no_cache=true)

If management wants a lifetime economics view, show warranty as an additional expected cost, not embedded in manufacturing material cost:

* My calculation — illustrative metric definition:
`Expected warranty-parts cost per machine sold = attributable warranty-parts consumption value for a comparable machine cohort / machines in that cohort`
Cohort age and incomplete warranty exposure matter; a single period's claims divided by that period's builds can be misleading.

### 5. Report what cannot be linked—without forcing it into a BOM
I would provide a reconciliation with separate rows for matched production consumption, matched spare/warranty consumption, identified non-product spend, purchases still in inventory or timing differences, and unresolved transactions. For unresolved spend, retain amount, plant, supplier, description, period, attempted match and reason (for example, blank item ID, ambiguous supplier SKU or no PO reference).

* My calculation — transparent coverage measure:
`Linked share = value of in-scope transaction lines confidently classified and linked / value of all in-scope transaction lines`
Report the unlinked value and percentage as well, broken out by plant and supplier. Do not distribute an "unknown" bucket across products just to make product costs sum to the spend cube.

Finally, "true cost of one product" needs a boundary. The formulas here establish direct purchased-material cost, with an actual or modeled label. A full manufactured unit cost also needs the applicable internal labor, machine time, overhead, scrap/rework, subcontracting and inventory/variance treatment. Public SAP product-cost reporting distinguishes costs per piece or lot and actual production costs and variances; a spend cube by itself does not supply those missing production costs.
