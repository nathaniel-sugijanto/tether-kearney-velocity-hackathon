## Our reading (ours, not Nick's words)

- Item 4: the status quo is manual work, paper forms Nick called "J9s" and Excel, on systems he described as 2005 tech. Clients want less manual work without an overhaul. Our tool should sit on top of exports from whatever they have and hand back files they can use. We do not know what a "J9" is; to ask.
- Item 5: Nick ranked two causes above the rest: superseded parts (suppliers give about six months notice and people forget it) and make versus buy (it changes over a part's life as capability and volume change). He suggested an alert system for supplier notifications and called it big. The usual industry name for a supplier's notice of a part change is a product change notification (PCN); that label is ours, not his.
- Item 5: he said all eight causes in our log are valid. Mismatched part numbers matter because wrong parts have to be quarantined.
- Item 6: 20 days for a full family "sounds correct" but depends on family size. Use 20 as the conservative figure and say how big a family it refers to. He asked us to work that out.
- Item 7: allocation depends on how granular the client's data is. Kearney builds spend from a volume forecast (volume grows x% over a year) and compares actuals against forecast. Consumables such as gaskets and bearings with no part numbers go under the radar.
- Item 8: "financial impact of one BOM" means full cost, not purchased material only. Duplicate parts are the low-hanging fruit. Lead times are "our biggest issue": they can go from 4 to 8 weeks after a logistics shake-up, freight can jump when sea moves to air, and being able to pivot between suppliers and see delays early is valuable. Suppliers do not notify as much as manufacturers would like.
- Item 9: in Nick's experience returns and warranty are documented separately, often in Excel, and reconciled by finance later. If the ERP already tracks warranty, our tool should use that data, not track it a second time. Analysis that reduces service needs would be a plus. He asked us to check whether ERP vendors include warranty tracking: see the answer in `Data Gathering Log.md` (source W1).
- Item 10: the order after the tie is (1) low-hanging fruit and variance reduction, (2) costing to get a baseline, (3) platforming. His analogy: first what a book costs, then how to make the book cost less and reuse it for other products.
